The whole project

In three sentences

  1. NASA is about to light the first fire ever on the Moon, in an experiment called FM², to learn whether spacecraft materials burn differently there.
  2. All 1,622 fire tests in NASA's public archive were done in orbit, with no gravity. There are none from the Moon and none from Mars.
  3. We built a website that organises and fact-checks all of that data, and a calculator called FLINT that predicts fire at any gravity. We sealed our predictions with a digital timestamp before the experiment flies.

The one idea

How a fire lives or dies

Every material has an oxygen limit.

It is the least oxygen the material needs to keep burning. Earth's air is 21% oxygen. A lower limit means a more dangerous material.

Gravity works like a fan.

On Earth, hot air rises and keeps feeding the flame fresh oxygen. On the Moon, gravity is six times weaker, so the fan runs at about half speed. In orbit there is no fan at all.

A flame dies in two ways.

Too much air flow blows it out, like blowing out a candle. Too little starves it, like a candle under a glass. So less gravity makes some materials burn more easily and others less. NASA saw exactly that at Moon gravity: three of five materials burned more easily, and two burned less.

What we found

What we found, and what comes next

Physics engine · FLINTEarth tests can predict the Moon.

Tuned only on NASA's Earth tests, FLINT predicted NASA's four Moon-gravity results to within 1.4 points of oxygen on average. A simple straight-line estimate misses by 2.3.

 Reading FM² · the sealed boxThe Moon experiment's sensor will read high.

In FM²'s closed box, the flame's own exhaust chokes it. When it dies, the sensor will show about 27% oxygen, but the flame really had about 26%.

 Flames in freefall · ISS dataCO₂ puts out a fire better than helium.

From 274 burning fuel drops on the Space Station (NASA's FLEX), CO₂ raises the oxygen a flame needs the most. Helium can backfire for some fuels.

 Pressurized roverIn a Moon rover, choose the materials.

Turning the fans off is not enough. Flooding with CO₂ would triple the cabin pressure. Venting works only with the crew in suits.

 Mission conceptThe next four fires to light.

The names

Every term, in plain words

FM²
Flammability of Materials on the Moon: a NASA experiment that will burn four fabric strips in a sealed box on the Moon, at 37, 33, 29 and 25% oxygen.
FLINT
Flammability Limits In Nonterrestrial gravity: our open-source Python engine. Give it a material, gravity, air and pressure, and it returns the oxygen limit.
FLEX
Flame Extinguishment Experiment: NASA lit tiny floating fuel drops on the International Space Station and watched them burn and go out.
CFI-G
A Space Station experiment with small gas flames. We tried to predict gas-leak fires from it; the data could not, and we say so.
PSI
Physical Sciences Informatics: NASA's free public library of space-experiment data, where our 1,622 records come from.
The NASA paper
ICES-2025-392 (2025): NASA tested five materials at Moon gravity in a spinning centrifuge inside a drop tower.
Oxygen limit
The least oxygen a material needs to keep burning. "Points" are percentage points: 21% to 22% is one point.
Referee
Our fact-checker. It checks that each quote exists in the NASA source, that the claim follows from it and that the numbers are right.
Sealed forecast
Our prediction locked with a digital timestamp and a SHA-256 fingerprint, like sealing an exam answer in an envelope.
Pressurized rover
A vehicle astronauts will drive and live in on the Moon, about the size of a small room, planned at 34% oxygen and 8.2 psi.
SI
Our term for the machine-learning tools used in this project. Their use is declared in the SI disclosure.
Freefall
Weightlessness, as on the Space Station. Flames there are round and slow because hot air does not rise.

Said plainly

What is still open

  • FM² has not flown yet, so the real test of our forecast is ahead.
  • FLINT is checked against four materials at Moon gravity. That is all NASA has published, and it is not yet reviewed by NASA.
  • Pressures other than 8.2 psia, and flames in orbit without a breeze, are model estimates, and the site marks them that way.
  • The forecast DOI, a volunteer blind test and independent sign-offs are pending.
