FLINT · Flammability Limits In Nonterrestrial gravity

When does a flame run out of oxygen?

A gentle flow can feed a flame. Too little flow lets a hot surface lose heat faster than the flame supplies it. Too much flow carries gases past the surface before they can react. FLINT calculates the oxygen needed to sustain a flame on both sides of that balance.

Flames in freefall

What stops a burning droplet?

On the ISS, a suspended droplet can burn inside a nearly spherical flame. NASA’s FLEX experiment varied the gas mixture and droplet size. FLINT fits the shrinking-droplet law to oxygen–nitrogen runs and tests how it transfers to atmospheres containing carbon dioxide or helium. 273 runs had the inputs needed for analysis. FLEX · PSI-69

Can a gas leak’s temperature and pressure predict ignition?

FLINT tested a simple ignition rule against normal and inverse jets in CFI-G. For normal jets its held-out Brier score is worse than the base rate. For inverse jets the score improves but accuracy falls. FLINT therefore withholds a gas-leak ignition rule. The archive lacks igniter energy and timing. CFI-G · PSI-159

Does turning the fans off extinguish a solid fire in orbit?

FLINT cannot answer this with the fans off: diffusion and motion of the flame can still bring oxygen to the surface. With a fan running, the solid limits are extrapolations for flames spreading with the flow. Flames opposed to the flow need a separate calibration. Compare orbital measurements from BASS, BASS-II and Saffire-I, II and III.
