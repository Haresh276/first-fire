# What FIRST FIRE learned from other challenge projects

**Review date: 14 November 2026.** The review covers the *Flame in Freefall* challenge, its named projects and two fire-modeling references. The public inventory records the repository revision, the README examined, the number of files found and topics detected in the text. Topic labels support discovery. They do not certify a project's implementation or scientific accuracy. ## Two changes you can try

**Compare a normal and an inverse flame.** Open the CFI-G pair in the Atlas. Read the chamber and burner oxygen on separate lines. In the inverse configuration the oxygen delivered by the burner describes the jet. Entering it as cabin oxygen would give a habitat ranking based on the wrong atmosphere. Open the two source rows to see what the investigators actually recorded. A second example traces a composite specimen's apparently positive burn result to the footnote that identifies the material that burned. Both comparisons preserve the original records and can be shared by URL. [Compare the two CFI-G records](#atlas?left=PSI-159%3A1%3A2&right=PSI-159%3A2%3A2) · [Compare the Saffire-II specimens](#atlas?left=PSI-99%3A1%3A3&right=PSI-99%3A1%3A8)

**Reverse the flow in a fixed cabin.** In Physics Lab, choose **Reverse the flow → Compare both inputs**. Saffire-I reported front speeds of 1.8 mm/s with the airflow and 1.3 mm/s against it. The PSI table supplies the run conditions, including the approximate oxygen entry for the second run. The paper supplies the speeds. Applied to the default scenario for 60 seconds, these rates give 10.8 cm and 7.8 cm of modeled travel. The distances and gas changes are calculations. The original experiment used the same specimen sequentially, so a stronger next test would counterbalance the order on matched fresh specimens. [Open Physics Lab](#physics) · [NASA experiment table](https://doi.org/10.60555/0t15-1z43) · [NASA presentation, slide 21](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170008805/downloads/20170008805.pdf#page=21)

## Which design ideas survived review?

| Source inspected | What we used or learned |
| --- | --- |
| MicroFire Atlas and Khalid Hasan's comparison route | Select two records directly. Show the fields that match, differ or are missing and keep a link back to each input. FIRST FIRE implements this against its own archived rows. [MicroFire comparison code](https://github.com/A-K-M-Asifuzzaman/MicroFire-Atlas/blob/acb9aaa69486154da657c61469d3a1886e4c12b1/apps/web/lib/compare.ts) · [Comparison interface](https://github.com/KhalidHasan03/Flame-in-Freefall/blob/44dc68271f2daa0c830f3883867548fea794c2d0/app/compare/page.js) |
| ALPINE IBEX and Flamebound | A numerical match on oxygen alone can hide a mismatch in pressure, sample or endpoint. Read the conditions together. FIRST FIRE exposes the individual fields and unreported measurements; their scoring algorithms were not imported. [ALPINE experimental-domain check](https://github.com/SoyebuzamanNaim/ALPINE_IBEX/blob/b113fa688d5b85f74ecf17d56f92f85f6af04649/src/envelope/guard.py) · [Flamebound comparison implementation](https://github.com/prattaybanik03/flamebound-flame-in-freefall/blob/a7adbbeb05f3c4cd6af3b7141c91e8ed09d8d9ac/web/src/lib/bcms.ts) |
| FLUXFIRE and EMBER | FLUXFIRE documents rules for showing source values and unknowns. EMBER's implemented data pipeline records the basis of oxygen and pressure. In the Saffire comparison FIRST FIRE preserves the approximate oxygen entry and identifies the absent pressure before applying a rate to a different cabin. [FLUXFIRE's published method](https://github.com/shomratmortaza/fluxfire-Orbital.Mind/blob/ea74d6249d636abe40220d1e907fac454487b294/SCIENTIFIC_INTEGRITY.md) · [EMBER data preparation](https://github.com/JaynaShirlie/ember/blob/28a06de91540478704a3a35e9939fd6643d8d221/pipeline/build.py) |
| FLEX droplet prediction | Its training code separates variables available before ignition from quantities learned during the burn. Otherwise a retrospective classifier can look like a forecaster. For FM² the four probabilities use pre-burn assumptions. Measured future outcomes belong in the scoring step. We did not transfer a droplet model to fabric. [Published training code](https://github.com/Anurag-k-007/flames-in-freefall/blob/effb8c841d31d7883c671df87df107ecd7fca9a1/train_ensemble.py) |
| SAFFIRE Vision | Calibration grids can help establish image scale and optical distortion. They cannot supply a flame speed. The Atlas distinguishes calibration and flow checks from normalized observations, even when they share the same source table. [Camera project scope and dataset](https://github.com/RadiyahShafiq-05/flame-in-freefall-saffire-vision/blob/43c1589937c1baddd464914e2569f87e20cb25bc/saffire-vision/README.md) |

## What the Word documents added

Two local Word documents informed the review. The challenge guide identified relevant comparison projects and explained how specimen geometry changes what a test can support. The FLUXFIRE research brief emphasized keeping original evidence in view while exploring a changed scenario. Their contents stay in the team's workspace.

The repository scan found 36 Word paths in 15 repositories. Of 34 genuine Word packages, 33 had extractable text, yielding 13 distinct bodies and 20 repeat copies. The remaining package held an image of a BRE ignition log. Reading the image revealed that the first round used a 25 mm burner and the second used both 25 mm and 50 mm burners. A comparison grouped only by round could hide that geometry change. This adds a fourteenth distinct document. Across these 14 there were nine investigation records, four team reports and one single-line template. Two other paths held expired download pointers. [BRE ignition log, embedded image](https://github.com/fswierta/nasa-hackathon/blob/098976e262b4fe173de5a58b118824992e910da7/project-data-1-pc1/data/documents/PSI-20/PSI-20_Science%20Documents_ACME-BRE_Test_Summary.docx)

## Scope, technical references and open questions

Five paginated GitHub searches covered `"flame in freefall"`, `"flames in freefall"` and `"flame-in-freefall"` in names, descriptions and READMEs, plus `"microgravity combustion" "space apps"` and `"microgravity fire" "space apps"` in READMEs. Their result counts were 275, 256, 275, 180 and 44 respectively, with considerable overlap. After deduplication and the named references, we inspected 310 public repository records, 303 complete default-branch file trees and 292 available READMEs. GitHub confirmed the other seven repositories were empty. The more detailed code and document readings are linked above. This is a dated search inventory. Other private, unindexed and differently named entries can exist. The connected Drive name searches returned no additional matching documents. [NASA Space Apps program repository](https://github.com/nasa/spaceapps/blob/c84cd8031d880947633610e8b58c006c12ab4595/README.md)

Cantera models thermodynamics, kinetics and transport. NIST's CFAST uses two zones for compartment fires. These provide separate comparisons for a future model review. FIRST FIRE currently computes well-mixed cabin averages and uses an experimental surrogate trained on its own solver. The recorded surrogate error measures agreement with that solver. Independent cabin sensor traces are still needed to measure physical error. [Cantera capabilities](https://github.com/Cantera/cantera/blob/25c1ab1c397e2991c77560b1ad5154919a12c733/README.rst) · [CFAST model scope](https://github.com/firemodels/cfast/blob/487cdc845e9b1b6b6b067d06e3aa192c7a6e27ce/README.md) · [FIRST FIRE simulation and validation design](world-model.md)

A human blind comparison with ordinary search remains open. Participants can already use the same question and source set under a timer, and assessors can score masked responses. The research question is whether FIRST FIRE improves the evidence cited, the decision or the time required. Software benchmarks alone cannot answer it. [Blind-test protocol](evaluation.md)
