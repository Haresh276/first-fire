# From a NASA observation to a testable decision

[Start a guided investigation](#session). Four tools answer different questions. A matched comparison describes archived flight observations. A transport model calculates a chosen scenario. A Bayesian design uses hypothetical outcomes. Research chat computes answers from the archived table. A session download preserves those distinctions alongside the inputs and source identities.

## Match before revealing the outcomes

The matched experiment tool restricts its comparison to a single FLEX fuel. Choose oxygen or pressure as the exposure, select two settings and constrain the other conditions, including droplet diameter and diluent composition. The algorithm pairs distinct rows using conditions known before the outcome. It first maximizes the number of pairs and then minimizes their total matching cost. Review the sample IDs, controls and tolerances before revealing the results. A locked protocol can still lose all its pairs when a stricter condition is applied. [Matched analysis](#transfer-lab) · [NASA FLEX report, Table IX](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20150023456)

At the default methanol settings, the 15% and 21% oxygen groups at a target 14.7 psia give three pairs. Their higher-minus-lower oxygen burn durations differ by −4.8, −3.3 and +12.9 seconds. Their average is +1.6 seconds. Requiring the same experimental day removes all three pairs. Repeated tests can share hardware, and burn durations can end through different mechanisms. Selecting by a predicted outcome would favor experiments that confirmed the prediction. [Inspect original FLEX data](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-69)

## Model a remote tracer sensor

The transport simulator represents two connected volumes at uniform concentration. Let A and B be the tracer masses in mg and Vₐ and Vᵦ the volumes in m³. Concentrations are A/Vₐ and B/Vᵦ. A source injects a constant mass per second during a finite pulse. A symmetric volumetric exchange Q transfers Q(A/Vₐ − B/Vᵦ) mg/s from A to B. A clean-air flow F into the remote zone exhausts F B/Vᵦ mg/s. A first-order sensor approaches the local concentration with time constant τ. At τ = 0 it follows that concentration instantly. The plot shows a five-minute run and the first crossing of the selected threshold. Inputs, units, mass balance, time series and limits are included with every download. [Run the simulation](#transport-lab)

The solver steps the mass balance every 0.1 seconds and integrates the sensor response exponentially over each step. Its tests check an analytic pulse response, zero exchange, nonnegative masses, conservation and refinement to 0.05 seconds. It models an inert tracer. Ignition, heat transport, buoyancy, aerosol deposition and detector calibration need independent measurements. Two-zone tracer fitting has a published basis. [Two-zone tracer decay study](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0668.1997.t01-1-00008.x)

The separate measured-repeat panel uses the four whole-room mixing times from Um et al. In the higher-flow, neutral-air setting the two runs took 174 and 206 seconds. In the lower-flow, heated-air setting they took 451 and 532 seconds. The room volume was 158 m³, the release lasted 20 seconds and the authors used a spatial relative standard deviation threshold of 20%. We approximate the sensor array with two equal sealed volumes. For inverse time constant λ = 4Q/V and pulse length d, the relative zone contrast at the end of the pulse is (1−exp(−λd))/(λd). It then decays exponentially. We fit λ using one reported time per setting, retain the setting and predict the other run. The absolute errors are 32 and 81 seconds. Copying the calibration time gives the same errors. Two repeat pairs test repeatability and cannot validate full concentration traces, sensor delay or spacecraft transfer. [Berkeley Lab experiment, §3.1 and Fig. 5](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2026-03/demonstration_of_a_novel_tracer_gas_method.pdf#page=5)

For timestamped concentration data, upload two run files to the trace fitter. Choose known conditions for each, fit on the first, then inspect residuals on the second alongside an independently fitted one-zone baseline. Keep the synthetic demonstration separate from measured data. [Calibration and held-out scoring](transport-fit.md)

## Choose the next experiment

The FM² Design Lab represents a single uncertain limiting oxygen percentage on a grid with 0.05-percentage-point bins. A burn sustains above this common limit. The likelihood includes a user-selected probability of recording the opposite binary outcome. Each hypothetical reading updates the weights. The calculation evaluates each reachable oxygen setting and, with two specimens, each possible second decision after the first reading. One objective maximizes expected information on the shared limit. The other reduces expected squared probability error for a selected planned burn. A contradictory sequence at zero reading error requires an explicit edit to the assumptions or readings. [Open experiment design](#design-lab) · [NASA FM² experiment design](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260001966)

At the defaults, two specimens with 5% reading error give an expected information gain of about 1.426 bits. The first setting is 26% oxygen. A sustained reading moves the next recommended setting to 25%; a negative reading moves it to 27%. These are model expectations under the stated assumptions. The separately signed four-burn forecast keeps its original probabilities and scoring protocol. [View the signed forecast](#forecast)

## Audit the chat and decision

The archive calculator binds a supported question to typed fields before applying filters and reductions. Blank values are excluded from numeric summaries. The result identifies the group size, available measurements and source rows. Visitors can inspect conditions such as burner oxygen separately from the chamber oxygen. A conversational answer about an experiment can also point the user to a source passage rather than perform a table calculation. [Open research chat](#chat) · [New archive question benchmark](science-chat-audit.md)

After starting a guided session, each tool contributes its current result to the tab's research record. Open the session at any point to inspect the recorded question and download a JSON copy. A partial run is identified in the export. The record contains modeled and hypothetical results, the supporting observation identifiers and the signed baseline fingerprint. It does not assign flight safety certification.
