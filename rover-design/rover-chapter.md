# Designing FIRST FIRE’s custom rover

**Team Code Blooded · NASA Space Apps 2026 · Research checked 15 November 2026**

A crew can drive an open lunar vehicle in pressure suits. Studying smoke in a shared cabin requires a different vehicle architecture. NASA’s Lunar Terrain Vehicle provides a reference for the chassis and crew transport role. JAXA’s pressurized rover provides a reference for a habitable enclosure, including pressure control and carbon-dioxide removal. FIRST FIRE lets a user combine those ideas, declare the physical parameters of the resulting vehicle and inspect what follows from those assumptions. Every assembled vehicle starts with **unverified interfaces**. [NASA lunar terrain vehicles](https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-phases/) · [JAXA cabin systems interview](https://humans-in-space.jaxa.jp/space-job/specialist/voice/detail/13.html)

This chapter has two reference sections. The first records what NASA and each international partner can contribute to the design. The second specifies the units, equations, operating bounds and acceptance tests for each tool. ## From two real missions to one hypothetical vehicle

The existing Rover Sandbox offers a JAXA-inspired 12 m³ cabin, an 800 kg NASA-inspired chassis and a battery. The existing Physics Lab uses Moon or Mars gravity, an imposed flame-front speed, a rigid sealed gas volume and a fuel-specific oxygen balance. Its Saffire comparison and transport lab already give users access to experiments. This package keeps the existing physics adapter independent. It adds editable subsystem choices and answers questions that arise *before* a fire calculation, such as how much oxygen an EVA costs and whether a sampling location can receive a prescribed release. [Current rover implementation](../site/physics-lab.js) · [Existing physical assumptions and experiments](../research/world-model.md) · [Existing transport model](../site/transport-lab.js)

**Measurement** means an instrument or experiment reported an observation under the listed conditions. **Specification** means an agency or manufacturer described a configuration, rating or design target. **Assumption** means the team or user supplied an input for this hybrid. **Simulation** means a calculation from those inputs. A published value can be excellent evidence about its source vehicle while the corresponding value for the hybrid remains unknown. For example, the JAXA/Toyota 2019 concept gave 13 m³ of living space. The builder’s 12 m³ is a free-gas-volume assumption after allowing for equipment and occupants. There is no measured conversion between those quantities here. [Original 2019 concept](https://global.toyota/en/newsroom/corporate/27059582.html) · [Parts and assumed settings](parts.json)

### What the surface missions contribute

- **Wheels and structure.** Apollo 17’s open electric rover traveled 35.9 km on the Moon. NASA gives 210 kg as the Apollo rover’s mass. On Mars, Perseverance is a 1,025 kg six-wheeled robot. FIRST FIRE can compare a four-wheel or six-wheel configuration under the same assumed total mass. To infer traction, an engineer also needs wheel geometry, slip, soil state and applied load. Single-wheel Earth tests on Rashid-like hardware demonstrate how to control those variables. [Apollo vehicle record](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/apollo_lrv.html) · [Perseverance specifications](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/rover-components/) · [Rashid wheel experiment, §2.1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11214-025-01164-8)
- **Power and sensing.** ISRO lists *whole-rover* generation of 50 W for Pragyan. Canada describes a rapid elemental survey by Curiosity’s APXS in roughly 5–10 minutes and a full analysis in 2–3 hours. Neither quantity gives the watt-hours required by a new sample-analysis package. Spain’s MEDA team provides a different kind of evidence, measuring the exterior environment at Jezero over specific sols. It can inform a Mars weather comparison while cabin pressure and temperature remain independent inputs. [ISRO rover specifications](https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3_Details.html) · [CSA instrument sequence](https://www.canada.ca/en/news/archive/2012/08/canada-contribution-mars-science-laboratory.html) · [MEDA report, pp. 1–3](https://cab.inta-csic.es/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/np_2021_04_06_parte_meteorologico_meda.pdf)
- **Fire experiments.** In Saffire-I, the investigators reported an average front speed of 1.8 mm/s with the flow and 1.3 mm/s against it for the wide SIBAL specimen at 20 cm/s. The table gives 21.5–21.7% oxygen for the first run and approximately 21.5% for the second. For a partial-gravity comparison, NASA reported different limiting oxygen levels for a 5-mil Mylar G film at 10.2 psia during short simulated lunar- and Mars-gravity drops. The first experiment measures front speed. The second uses a duration-dependent survival criterion. Neither measures a new rover liner. [Saffire-I, slide 21](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170008805/downloads/20170008805.pdf#page=21) · [Original Saffire-I table](evidence/files/saffire-i.csv) · [Partial-gravity experiments, Table I](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20130010991/downloads/20130010991.pdf)

## How to assemble a vehicle

Choose one template in each slot, then give it a name and edit its assumptions. A family such as “Mars-inspired six-wheel set” describes an arrangement. Document all installed equipment in the mass and power budgets, including harnesses, mounts, packaging and consumables. Count each item once. The prototype never adds the mass or power of a complete source rover to a payload budget. [Part catalogue](parts.json) · [Scenario field definitions and bounds](scenario.schema.json)

| Subsystem | What the builder can answer | Evidence needed for the assembled vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| Cabin | Open deck or sealed free volume. For a sealed cabin, compute gas inventory and initial oxygen partial pressure. | Occupied and free-volume survey, pressure boundary, penetrations and measured leakage. Open decks require personal suit support. |
| Chassis and wheels | Mass budget, static weight and equal wheel loads on level ground. | Mount drawings, centre of mass, rated loads, wheel-soil tests, braking, fatigue and rollover envelope. |
| Payload | Choose a contact spectrometer, weather suite, navigation cameras, borehole instrument, regolith package or cargo. | Mount, field of view, calibration, occupied work area, data bus, thermal strap, supply voltage, start-up and operating loads. |
| Electrical and thermal | Solar production, energy shortfall, curtailment, and a radiator’s signed heat margin at a specified surface temperature. | Pack and bus curves, shadow time history, conversion losses, radiator views, coating properties, heat-path conductance and rejection tests. |
| Life support and EVA | Oxygen use, CO₂ removal demand, regulated leak makeup, airlock gas loss and a suitport dead-volume alternative. | Crew work profile, scrubber breakthrough, regulator/inventory limits, dead volume, pump curve, recovery efficiency, purge and dust tests. |
| Detection and suppression | How placement, flow and sensor delay affect an ideal tracer crossing. How much suppressant a commanded release consumes. | Aerosol-specific detector response, false alarms, airflow map, access behind panels, agent distribution, electrical compatibility, extinction and reignition tests. |
| Materials and sourcing | Coupon chemical potential, oxygen demand and raw stock required after trim. An unknown laminate withholds chemistry. | Exact grade, lot, coatings, thickness, mounting, supplier documentation, process yields and configuration-matched test reports. |

Fit requires more than matching units. For instance, a spectrometer on an arm needs clearance to contact a target, power during acquisition and a path to reject heat. The public CSA measurement times confirm a duty-cycle decision but do not establish any of those interfaces. The **unverified** flag therefore follows the design through export. An integration team can close individual gaps with drawings, measurements or tests tied to a particular revision. [CSA APXS](https://www.canada.ca/en/news/archive/2012/08/canada-contribution-mars-science-laboratory.html) · [Agency-specific evidence limits](agency-register.md)

## Environment and fire controls

**Choose where measurements are made.** Moon and Mars use nominal surface gravity, Earth provides a supported ground-test case, and Orbit represents an unrestrained, freely falling vehicle. Its mass persists while sustained support force vanishes. A three-axis accelerometer measures specific force. Only a stationary, supported reading is compared with the selected surface gravity. Acceleration, vibration or free fall can make a single reading ambiguous. [NASA on orbital free fall](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-is-microgravity-grades-5-8/) · [IMU rule and acceptance tests](tool-reference.md)

**Choose the exterior and interior separately.** Day/night sets direct sunlight on or off. The user can enter an exterior pressure and an effective longwave temperature seen by the radiator. The latter represents radiation from the surroundings weighted by exposure. Sunlight on the radiator and the PV panel depends on their separate projected areas. Neither a lunar surface extreme nor a Mars weather reading fixes the sealed cabin’s gas temperature. The model covers a constant interval. Ground convection, terrain conduction, dust cover and orbital eclipse schedules require additional site or mission data. [NASA lunar surface conditions](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/) · [NASA thermal balance, §7.1](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/7-soa-thermal-2024.pdf)

**Choose the source and detector positions separately.** A release in the equipment bay can go undetected in the crew space during a short observation. An equal-volume, closed two-zone model transfers a prescribed passive tracer through fan-driven exchange. A separate duct estimate compares fan direction with the assumed ignition end of a strip. Reversing the fan changes the concurrent/opposed label. The symmetric exchange model is insensitive to direction and solves only the bulk mixing question. Particle size, buoyancy, filtration, deposition and source strength would be needed to predict alarm response to an actual fire. [NASA fire-protection brief, p. 8](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-008-fire-protection.pdf#page=8) · [Saffire-I flow comparison](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170008805/downloads/20170008805.pdf#page=21)

**Budget losses against a declared boundary.** The cabin starts as dry O₂/N₂ at specified pressure and temperature. The leak and airlock estimates assume a regulator and external reserves maintain those conditions. One EVA cycle vents unrecovered gas in the isolated lock volume. The suitport option cycles a much smaller assumed interface volume. An exterior pressure higher than cabin pressure withholds the outward-flow calculation. Crew consumption and carbon-dioxide removal are capacity estimates. A cabin pressure or CO₂ time history under failure would need valve, tank, controller and loop dynamics. [NASA EVA access study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230002484) · [NASA reference metabolic rates, Table 3-24](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210024855/downloads/BVAD_2.15.22-final.pdf#page=63)

## How to source and compare materials

For an Earth laboratory coupon, Cytiva publishes nominal 180 µm thickness and 87 g/m² basis weight for Whatman Grade 1 filter paper. Röchling identifies Polystone G HD natural extruded, article 590976, as PE-HD and lists stabilizer and additives. These are traceable candidate stocks. Neither vendor document measures the prospective lot in the rover’s chosen atmosphere. The model uses the generic **net** heats for cellulose and polyethylene in NBS Table 1. A swap changes the assumed chemistry, while a supplier selection records where a specimen could come from. [Cytiva product guide, PDF p. 16](https://info.cytivalifesciences.com/rs/614-VFU-334/images/Whatman-filtration-product-guide.pdf#page=16) · [Röchling grade and constituents, p. 1](https://www.roechling.com/fileadmin/assets/Product%20Handling%20Information%20Sheet%20Polystone%C2%AE%20G%20HD%20natural%20extruded%20590976%20EN.pdf) · [NBS Table 1, printed p. 66](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/TN/nbstechnicalnote1128.pdf#page=78)

Record supplier or feedstock origin, grade, lot, stock thickness, net dry mass and assumed usable yield. During a physical trial, measure dimensions and mass after conditioning. Record the mount, adhesive, backing, orientation, pressure, oxygen fraction, gravity and flow alongside the outcome. Use the exact installed assembly in the applicable materials review. NASA-STD-6001B Change 3, Test 1 gives a concrete comparison using bottom ignition on vertical specimens in the anticipated worst-case atmosphere. A commercial datasheet, a burn in a different orientation and a prediction from ideal chemistry answer different questions. [NASA-STD-6001B Change 3, §7.1](https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/B-w/CHANGE-3/3/2025_06_25_NASA-STD-6001B_w_Change_3_FINAL_Admin-Change-FINAL.pdf#page=27)

Reclaimed stock, lunar feedstock and Martian feedstock are available as theoretical origins. Net coupon mass divided by usable yield gives a bookkeeping feed requirement. Establishing a local source also requires composition, an extraction and processing route, energy, consumables, waste and coupon-property evidence. The builder grants no local-production or flight qualification on the strength of a selected origin. [Sourcing rule and bounds](tool-reference.md) · [Input provenance fields](scenario.schema.json)

## Worked hybrid · Code Blooded field lab

The team’s hypothetical assembly uses a pressurized cabin inspired by JAXA, a chassis inspired by NASA’s open LTV, six wheels inspired by Perseverance, and a Canadian APXS-inspired payload. A theoretical 4 kWh pack, projected 2 m² solar panel, cabin scrubber, pump-recovery airlock, sampling detector and water-mist inventory complete the design. The chassis is assumed to weigh 800 kg, other installed hardware 650 kg, cargo 40 kg and two occupants 85 kg each with worn gear. Total modeled mass is 1,660 kg. No agency supplied or approved this combined bill of materials. [Lunar baseline input](examples/lunar-cellulose.json) · [Source roles and scope](parts.json)

**A. Lunar day, cellulose.** Use 12 m³ of free cabin gas at 70 kPa absolute, 30% O₂ and 295 K. The initial oxygen partial pressure is 21.0 kPa. Both occupants perform nominal activity for four hours. One 1.5 m³ airlock cycle recovers 75% of its depressurization gas. A 30 L/s fan exchanges air between two equal zones. For a 60 mg tracer release in the equipment bay, the crew-space sampling threshold is 2 mg/m³ and the assumed delay is 5 s. The exterior longwave sink is set to 245 K and direct solar flux to 900 W/m². These are scenario assumptions. [Baseline input](examples/lunar-cellulose.json) · [Crew sizing reference, Table 3-24](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210024855/downloads/BVAD_2.15.22-final.pdf#page=63)

**B. Material-only swap.** Replace the dry cellulose coupon with ideal polyethylene. Hold its prescribed reacting mass at 10 g and leave the environment and equipment unchanged. The generic net heat increases from 152 to 434 kJ. Stoichiometric O₂ demand increases from 0.370 to 1.069 mol. At the assumed 80% usable yield, both options require 12.5 g of feedstock. The tracer experiment still has the same input release, so it gives the same crossing time. Measuring an actual smoke yield for each stock would allow an experiment linking material choice to detector response. [Swap-only input](examples/lunar-pe.json) · [NBS heat table](evidence/files/tn1128.pdf#page=78)

**C. Changed conditions, same polyethylene.** Move the assembly to Mars at night. Set the exterior to 0.636 kPa, effective radiative sink to 185 K, cabin to 56.54 kPa and 34% O₂, exchange to 10 L/s and airlock cycles to two. Direct solar generation is zero in darkness. This is a combined environmental and operational sensitivity case. Attribution of any one effect requires holding the other inputs fixed. [Mars-night input](examples/mars-night-pe.json)

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Static load per wheel, level ground | 448.2 N | 448.2 N | 1,026.4 N |
| Oxygen partial pressure at start | 21.00 kPa | 21.00 kPa | 19.22 kPa |
| O₂ required by crew plus regulated gas makeup over four hours | 391.3 g | 391.3 g | 474.0 g |
| Gas lost through airlock cycles | 10.70 mol | 10.70 mol | 17.09 mol |

The Mars case illustrates why cabin pressure and oxygen percentage both belong on the control panel. Raising the fraction to 34% while reducing total pressure lowers the initial oxygen partial pressure. It also shows why the observation window belongs beside a detector result. At 120 s the Mars-case remote concentration is 1.65 mg/m³, below the 2 mg/m³ threshold. The output preserves this distinction from zero tracer or a broken detector. Every figure in this table is a simulation; the source records and their conditions remain available separately. [Exact outputs](examples/results.json) · [Executable model](prototype/model.mjs)

## Drive the hybrid on a NASA reference route

NASA’s Mars 2020 map records a 36.39 m Perseverance drive on sol 15 from motion counter `3_110` to `3_386`. Its 47 longitude/latitude/elevation triples preserve a flight path. The workbench projects the horizontal positions into a local frame and reduces the path to three commanded waypoints with 0.15 m simplification tolerance. Source positions carry no individual timestamps or battery observations in this record. [NASA/JPL location map](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/location-map/) · [Original GeoJSON feature](terrain/perseverance-sol15.geojson) · [Projection and verification](terrain/README.md)

**Team assumptions.** Use the 1,660 kg hybrid with the polyethylene coupon on a flat Mars course. Set wheel command to 1 m/s, slip to 5%, rolling resistance to 0.05, friction coefficient to 0.5 and drive efficiency to 75%. The pack starts at 3,000 Wh with 500 W of base load. The day case produces 225 W of solar electricity. The night case produces 0 W. Both start from the same charge. [Editable night plan](examples/jezero-sol15-plan.json) · [Traverse inputs and equations](traverse-reference.md)

| Simulated quantity | Day | Night |
|---|---:|---:|
| Waypoints reached | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| Distance traveled | 36.295 m | 36.295 m |
| Elapsed simulation time | 40.5 s | 40.5 s |
| Drive / base energy | 4.421 / 5.625 Wh | 4.421 / 5.625 Wh |
| Solar input | 2.531 Wh | 0 Wh |
| Final charge | 2,992.485 Wh | 2,989.954 Wh |

The 2.531 Wh difference equals 225 W × 40.5 s ÷ 3,600. The two passes use the simulated controller's waypoint tolerance and paths. Wheel slip, drive time and power require vehicle-specific observations. An independent trial can compare displacement or battery-change error over matched times in the workbench. [Field comparison procedure](OPERATIONS.md) · [Source and energy regression](prototype/jezero-plan.test.mjs)

A second exercise uses a 5.12 km lunar terrain tile centered near 89.50006° S. Its heights come from NASA LRO’s LOLA product at 80 m spacing. In the 3D workbench, a team can follow a projected profile and examine how the measured-scale terrain changes static slope and drive demand for its *assumed* rover. The interpolation supplies broad topography. Wheel-scale rock clearance, soil response and rollover margins need finer site and vehicle data. ## What would justify stronger claims

| Claim to investigate | Experiment and acceptance evidence |
|---|---|
| An installed wheel can support or propel the hybrid | Apply the design normal load and measure deflection, traction, sinkage and motor torque over a documented soil, slope and slip range. Compare against a repeat trial on the same rig. The static load alone cannot set a climbing limit. |
| A fan setting gives a known time to detection | Release a calibrated nonreacting tracer in a known bay, measure fan exchange and concentration at the detector, then repeat in the other location. Fit on one trial and evaluate the second against sensor uncertainty. Characterize the optical response separately for each representative aerosol. |
| A material swap changes actual fire behavior | Test matched, traceable coupons and installed stacks at specified pressure, O₂, flow, gravity and ignition geometry. Report front position, mass consumed and uncertainty separately from ideal chemical potential. |
| A suitport saves a specified gas mass | Instrument the trapped volume, pressures, temperatures and recovery tank. Compare net tank mass change per complete cycle, including seal leakage and suit purge. |
| A discharge extinguishes a particular fire | Establish agent arrival at the ignition site, extinction endpoint, reignition observation, cleanup and electrical effects under the intended conditions. The prototype supplies a mass balance for the available charge. |

The acceptance tests shipped here check the equations, declared bounds and reporting behavior. Physical validation requires the corresponding independent measurements. [Tool contracts](tool-reference.md) · [Model tests](prototype/model.test.mjs) · [Integration handoff](HANDOFF.md)
