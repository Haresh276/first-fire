FLINT · Flammability Limits In Nonterrestrial gravity

When does a flame run out of oxygen?

A gentle flow can feed a flame. Too little flow lets a hot surface lose heat faster than the flame supplies it. Too much flow carries gases past the surface before they can react. FLINT calculates the oxygen needed to sustain a flame on both sides of that balance.

F7 · FM² chamber forecast

How much of the lunar test strip would burn?

Oxygen falls as a sealed burn adds carbon dioxide and water vapour. Both cool the flame. This is why a chamber sensor at extinction can read more oxygen than the fabric’s flame-equivalent limit. Each run here starts with fresh gas.

F12–F14 · ISS experiments

Flames in freefall

What stops a burning droplet?

On the ISS, a suspended droplet can burn inside a nearly spherical flame. NASA’s FLEX experiment varied the gas mixture and droplet size. FLINT fits the shrinking-droplet law to oxygen–nitrogen runs and tests how it transfers to atmospheres containing carbon dioxide or helium. $flex_n runs had the inputs needed for analysis. FLEX · PSI-69

$suppressants

Carbon dioxide raises the extinction threshold for both fuels. Helium raises it slightly for methanol but lowers it for heptane. These hot-flame calculations apply to orbital droplets within the FLEX range. Cool flames and lunar or Martian droplets are outside the model.

$flex_validation
Helium bias and heptane scatter. The transferred methanol model overpredicts helium burning rates by $helium_bias%. The heptane rate has $heptane_scatter% mean absolute error even in the ordinary oxygen–nitrogen training runs. Treat heptane rate estimates as order-of-magnitude.
$flex_figures

Can a gas leak’s temperature and pressure predict ignition?

FLINT tested a simple ignition rule against normal and inverse jets in CFI-G. For normal jets its held-out Brier score is worse than the base rate. For inverse jets the score improves but accuracy falls. FLINT therefore withholds a gas-leak ignition rule. The archive lacks igniter energy and timing. CFI-G · PSI-159

$cfig

Does turning the fans off extinguish a solid fire in orbit?

FLINT cannot answer this with the fans off: diffusion and motion of the flame can still bring oxygen to the surface. With a fan running, the solid limits are extrapolations for flames spreading with the flow. Flames opposed to the flow need a separate calibration. Compare orbital measurements from BASS, BASS-II and Saffire-I, II and III.

What changes inside a pressurized rover?

In a small cabin, a material’s oxygen margin tells only part of the story. Compare it with the heat and carbon dioxide that build up after ignition, and with the pressure or suppressant dose needed to stop the fire. The lunar solid limits use the ICES calibration with conflicting figure readings.
