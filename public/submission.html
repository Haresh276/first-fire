Project Name

FIRST FIRE — A Research Desk for Spacecraft Fire Safety

High-Level Project Summary

Would a rover's fabric or tape keep burning after the cabin fans stop? FIRST FIRE helps researchers find which NASA experiments can answer questions like this and where a new test is needed. Our dashboard ranks studies on fire prevention, detection and suppression, and links each study to its source. We tested our fire model by hiding one of four simulated Moon-gravity measurements at a time. On NASA's summary plot, its oxygen estimates missed by 1.4 percentage points on average, versus 2.3 for a straight line. The individual plots reverse that ranking. We published both results and recorded four predictions for NASA's planned Moon experiment before its results arrive.

Detailed Project Description

We built a browser dashboard and a Python fire model. The dashboard indexes 1,622 source rows from 15 NASA investigations. A researcher chooses a question, opens the original measurements, and sees which experiments used comparable conditions. For example, three space station smoke tests showed a signal in one detector while the separate Shuttle alarm stayed silent. The power records explain why the silence alone cannot establish that the alarm missed the smoke.

In the Fire Lab, change the cabin atmosphere and inspect how the estimated oxygen limit for a burning material changes. The FLINT model was tested against four materials under simulated lunar gravity. Its average error was 1.4 percentage points of oxygen on the paper’s summary figure. A straight-line estimate averaged 2.3. Using values from the individual figures reverses that ranking. We publish both comparisons. Our FM² decoder lets researchers try hypothetical burn lengths before NASA measures flame spread on the Moon.

The material observer carries cabin settings into the physics and rover simulators. It compares recipes with reference materials and shows measured flight results for cotton–fiberglass fabric. An English and Telugu classroom exercise lets students in Amaravati trace an alarm reading to the original experiment. The India case asks which measurements an engineer would need before applying the space findings to an oxygen-enriched ward. Use of Super Intelligence (SI)

We used OpenAI ChatGPT and Codex for source discovery, coding, writing and tests. Anthropic Claude assisted with FLINT, its tests and figures, the rover case and presentation materials. The research tools use models to find passages and help check quotations. Experimental measurements come from the cited data. The demo uses macOS speech synthesis for English narration. The rover concept image is SI-generated. Read the SI disclosure and credits.

NASA Data

We indexed the original tables for 15 investigations in NASA’s Physical Sciences Informatics archive. FLEX supplies fuel-droplet measurements from the International Space Station. SAME-R supplies detector signals and alarm records. Saffire-II supplies a comparison of solid materials in a spacecraft fire. NASA’s reduced-gravity material tests underpin FLINT, and NASA’s FM² experiment design defines our forecast conditions.
