# FIRST FIRE: why these instruments are in the judging demo

Team Code Blooded · 15 November 2026. Decisions for the public interactive release. This is a feature-selection and evidence review, not a prediction of an award.

## What the challenge asks

The official [Flame in Freefall brief](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/flame-in-freefall/) asks for an interactive dashboard that helps people find, rank and interpret NASA combustion research for crewed environments. It specifically discusses pressurized rovers, oxygen concentration, airflow, solid specimens, smoke detection and fire suppression. Terrestrial applications are optional. Rocket-engine and propellant combustion are generally outside the stated research scope. A rover is useful here as a cabin whose assumptions can be changed; driving it across a landscape does not answer a fire question. A copy of the published brief was captured in the project on 14 November, and the live brief was checked again on 15 November.

The national/global [judging guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/) and [submission guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/) identify impact, creativity, validity, relevance and presentation. They do not say a model is better because it uses more AI. Our engineering decision is to put a hand-operated test, traceable inputs, a counterexample and an export before the archive search. These are global criteria, not an inferred Amaravati scoring formula. The [Amaravati event listing](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/local-events/amaravati/) was consulted; no local Round 2 scoring weights were used.

## What earns a place on screen

| Feature | Decision | Evidence and reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NASA source rows and ranked findings | Front and center. Pick an endpoint and specimen, inspect direct or related studies, then open NASA or the bundled record. | The brief names [NASA PSI](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/search?q=combustion) as the data source. A droplet, smoke particle and fabric front are different endpoints. Ties use editorial order, not fabricated probabilities. Seven studies form the curated guide; the larger Atlas and condition-matching tools remain available. |
| Pressurized rover, specimen and cabin physics | Primary hands-on experiment. Begin with the two orbital source rates; allow reversal, changing mass, chemistry, oxygen, enclosure, volume and a custom speed. | [Saffire-I run conditions](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-98) and the [investigators’ rate plot, slide 21](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170008805/downloads/20170008805.pdf#page=21) support the source inputs. The NASA table gives specimen and setup; the plotted rates come from the report. The shared 16 m³ rover is an assumed scenario. |
| Two useful failures | Keep both: an open deck cannot generate sealed-cabin gas results; a manually edited rate cannot cite NASA as its measurement. | [NASA’s Saffire overview](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/spacecraft-fire-safety-demonstration-saffire.pdf) describes specific flight specimens and apparatus. Changing from orbit to a rover introduces an extrapolation. An edited rate may accidentally equal the published rate and still be a user assumption. |
| User, question, state and device awareness | Keep only when it changes what the user sees or does: explanation level, corrected intent, reproducible paired settings, optional computation or sensible refusal. | The team master, pp. 3–6, describes the judging exchange. The interim master, F2–F5, details device hints, privacy, instrument routing, sources and receipts. Exercises retain identical physics under different levels and render tiers. Device hints are not physical measurements or calibration; an inferred reader level is correctable. |
| Liquid network | Optional comparison only in the training envelope, with a deterministic baseline. Do not present it as learned orbital or lunar truth. | The team’s [model and validation notes](world-model.md) describe a surrogate trained on synthetic solver outputs. By contrast, [NASA’s partial-gravity experiments](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250010653) and [FM² planning](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260001966) address specific experimental gravity conditions. |
| Detection and suppression | Retain as alternative instruments, not as hidden assumptions in the flame-front model. | In [SAME-R](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-101), smoke generation, optical measurement and a powered alarm are distinct; the [NIST/NASA detector study](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2018/05/nistnasa-study-shows-one-detector-doesnt-fit-all-smoke-spacecraft) documents material-dependent response. [FLEX](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-69) and the [investigator report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20150023456/downloads/20150023456.pdf#page=189) distinguish visible extinction from later chemistry. [BASS-II](https://psi.nasa.gov/physci/repo/data/investigations/PSI-25) supplies a separate solid-fuel line of evidence. |
| Lunar forecasting, full rover parts, Earth analogs | Retain as secondary resources. Keep a forecast distinct from an observation, and a component assumption distinct from a flight-qualified part. | Planned FM² measurements are prospective, not results. The track allows Earth applications. A relevant extension names the transferable mechanism and the missing measurement; a general vehicle catalogue is not a substitute. |
| Terrain traversal, range, wheel/traction comparisons, generic power budgeting, competitor/library audits and model-provider scoreboards | Excluded from the principal judging flow. Project-internal evaluation and technical provenance remain available separately. | None of these, in isolation, ranks or interprets a combustion experiment. Gravity/load is retained as a negative control. Comparison with general-purpose prompting is a testable study question, not a demonstrated win. |

## A fair live test

1. Open the rover experiment. At 50 s with the default 16 m³ closed cabin and 10 g strip, switch from the concurrent to the opposed Saffire-I rate. Travel changes from 9.0 to 6.5 cm. Follow both the row and the rate publication. Both orbital burns used the same specimen in sequence. Do not attribute the orbital difference solely to direction.
2. Enter a speed yourself. The model should continue to compute while the flight-rate attribution disappears. Change only the gravity; a support load can change without making the imposed flame-front law gravity sensitive.
3. Open the cabin, then try an unknown material. Missing model inputs should cause an explicit withheld result. A missing reading is not a zero.
4. Switch reading depth or display budget. The explanation and available detail can change, but the deterministic physical result must not. Freeze a baseline, export the paired trial, change the settings, and import the file to recompute.
5. Use the evidence guide to select smoke detection and a droplet specimen. No direct case among the seven is a legitimate response. Choose polymer instead and open SAME-R. A separate investigator can inspect the original run and detector state.

## Limits and attribution

The team authored the project concept and the experimental questions. AI-assisted implementation was used for interface code, deterministic routing and testing and for drafting the research summaries. The original NASA tables and reported orbital rates were not generated by AI. The dashboard does not establish hardware suitability, an ignition threshold, causal isolation of flow direction or a win rate against a general-purpose assistant. A fair comparative claim still needs a same-source, blinded evaluation with independent participants. These implementation tests are not physical-device trials or laboratory validation.
